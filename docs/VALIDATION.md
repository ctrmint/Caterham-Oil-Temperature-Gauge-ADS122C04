# Verification plan

## Tests already exercised in this package

- C++ unit tests: ratiometric resistance calculation, ground-offset cancellation and ground-fault window, broken ground-sense wire, uncalibrated lockout, open/short/supply/ADC faults; with the synthetic demo table, interpolation, below/above-range handling, alarm debounce, hysteresis (Red → Amber → Normal), above-range critical alarm, alarm retention through intermittent faults, and the below-range hold (a latched alarm is kept, with status `BelowRange` and no temperature, until `kBelowRangeHoldMs` after the first raw sample colder than the table (fault samples in between neither pause nor restart that time), then clears; no hold when no alarm is latched).
- Display host suites: `tests/display_text_test.cpp` (unitless reading text, `<min` / `>max`, °C to °F conversion and rounding, footer and fault text, `ohm` when uncalibrated), `tests/sweep_model_test.cpp` (the lit-segment count and its cap at the table maximum when above range; the 30–130 °C / 86–266 °F scale and its labels and their visibility; no segments when uncalibrated, below range or on a fault; the seven-segment table; the graduated colours, their stops and half-way band boundaries, no hard steps, each segment's colour, and the reading colour never less severe than the latched alarm, amber on a fault or when uncalibrated; Sweep's three-cell reading with the `<` / `>` sign split off and `--` for anything longer) and `tests/layout_choice_test.cpp` (layout encode/decode with Classic fallback, long-press detector). The display-text and sweep-model suites also run with `-DGAUGE_DEMO`. The colour rules are host-tested; that `GaugeDisplay` and the layouts apply them (digits, status text, Classic's arc, each lit segment) is covered only by the human demo-build check below.
- Python unit tests: monotonic calibration generator, minimum of 5 points, comment lines, malformed-row rejection, the example file being unusable, generated calibration flag. For the sender graph (`tests/test_sender_curve.py`): the committed `docs/sender-curve.svg` matches its inputs; it follows threshold changes, measured points and a verified table, and never plots the unverified default; the reference curves are rejected by the calibration tool; and non-monotonic reference data is rejected.
- Stripboard layout tests (`tests/test_veroboard_usb_v1.py`): the layout's checker (`generator/vero.py`) finds no problems, so its strips, cuts and links make exactly the circuit, Rp's Kelvin tap is right, every part sits at its datasheet size and lead spacing (resistors 10 mm or more), and nothing touches; the checker catches a layout broken on purpose (a missing cut or link, a short resistor, overlapping parts, a lead in a cut, a link under a part); the circuit is through-hole-usb-v1's; and the generated files are current.
- PCB design tests (`tests/test_surface_mount_v1.py`, `tests/test_through_hole_v1.py`, `tests/test_through_hole_usb_v1.py`): every generated file matches its generator, with nothing unlisted; the measurement and protection topology follows WIRING.md (nothing in series with the sender, the Kelvin A0 tap, two-stage clamped taps, the bias on the harness side); the display connector matches the firmware's pins and address; the through-hole boards are single-sided and through-hole only, with multiple ground pins; through-hole-v1 has its 12 V input chain, and through-hole-usb-v1 takes only the display's 3.3 V and tracks its harness ground return to the display's ground; and every part has a part number, with the connectors' mating parts. ERC and DRC run in each board's `build.sh`, not in CI.

Run: `./scripts/test_host.sh`.
